    The bet

    Start with mid-sized credit unions. Replace their collection agency.

    US enterprise is crowded, as you said. The middle of the credit union market has the same pain, public data on every account, and a pricing model that already matches yours.

    2x

    Credit union loan losses are running near 0.80% of loans, about double the 0.40 to 0.50% they used to hover around.1

    35%

    What a collection agency keeps in one worked credit union example. The credit union nets 6.4 cents per dollar written off.2

    $20+

    Cost of one live conversation when a human collector dials: $4 to $6 per attempt, and only about 1 in 5 calls connect.3

    Why it holds together

    Market and productLosses are up and collectors are hard to hire.4 AI can call every account, including the small balances agencies skip.
    Product and channelEvery credit union files its loan losses with the regulator each quarter. Outbound can open with the buyer's own numbers.
    Channel and modelA success fee means no budget request. A cold call can end in a free pilot instead of a procurement cycle.
    Model and marketCredit unions already pay agencies a share of recoveries. Same model, smaller cut, more accounts worked.

    Ideal customer profile

    A $500M to $5B credit union with rising losses and an agency on contract.

    Big enough to write off millions a year. Small enough that the CFO can approve a no-risk pilot without a committee.

    The profile

    Size$500M to $5B in assets. 748 US credit unions are above $500M and only 21 are above $10B.1
    Loan mixHeavy in used auto and credit cards. Subprime borrowers now hold 16% of credit union auto balances, the fastest-rising share of any loan type.2
    Credit trendDelinquency above the 0.69% median and rising.3
    OperationsSmall collections team that already sends written-off accounts to an agency. my assumption
    MembersLow-income designated or Hispanic-serving. More than 134 credit unions hold the Juntos Avanzamos designation for serving Hispanic members.4
    BuyersVP or Director of Collections feels the workload. The CFO feels the agency fee and signs.

    Why the middle

    • Under $500M is too small. These are 83% of credit unions, they are losing members, and they cannot absorb new technology costs.5
    • Over $10B is enterprise. Long procurement, and the best-funded AI vendors are already there. Salient works with more than five of the top ten US auto lenders.6
    • The middle is ready to buy. 59% of credit unions have generative AI in production. The top barrier to more is compliance uncertainty, so the pitch has to lead with it.7

    Who to skip

    • Commercial-loan-heavy credit unions. Their losses sit in business loans, which are not collected by phone and text.
    • Credit unions that sell their written-off debt. There is no agency fee to undercut.

    Credit unionsStart herePublic data, a familiar pricing model, and about 700 accounts in the size band.
    Buy-here-pay-here car dealersSecondRegular dealers hand loans to banks. These dealers finance the car themselves and collect every payment, often weekly. Serious auto delinquency is at a 15-year high.8

    MortgageSkip for nowA success fee does not fit monthly mortgage payments, servicing has its own heavy rules, and Salient is already expanding into it.6

      Talk tracks

      What I would actually say.

      Built on the 30 Minutes to President's Club playbook: own the cold call, name one painful problem, and sell the test drive instead of the product.1 Green text is filled in from each account's public filing. Lakeshore is a made-up example.

      Cold call to a collections leader

      OpenerContext, own it, ask permission
      Hi Dana, it's James. I was reading Lakeshore's latest call report before I dialed. Full honesty, this is a cold call. Can I take 30 seconds to say why I called, and then you decide if we keep going?
      ProblemSpecific enough to be wrong
      You wrote off about $8.6 million in loans over the last year, up from the year before. At most credit unions your size, those accounts go to an agency that keeps around a third of whatever comes back and only really works the larger balances. The small ones never get a call.
      AskSell the test drive
      We use AI voice and text to work every one of those accounts, for a success fee below an agency's. Would 15 minutes next week be worth it to see what a 30-day test on one old batch could look like? If it collects nothing, it costs nothing.

      Permission-based openers booked meetings on 11.2% of calls in Gong's study of 300 million cold calls, against 2.2% for "Did I catch you at a bad time?"2

      First email, to the CFO. Under 80 words.

      Subject: $8.6M written off

      Dana, Lakeshore wrote off about $8.6M in loans last year, per your call report.

      If an agency recovers a tenth of that and keeps a third, roughly $300K goes to fees.

      We work the same accounts with AI voice and text, including the small balances, for a lower success fee. Nothing collected, nothing owed.

      Open to a 30-day test on one batch your agency has already returned?

      Voicemail and LinkedIn note

      Voicemail, 15 seconds. Dana, James here. I sent you a short note about Lakeshore's written-off loans and what your agency keeps. No need to call back, the email has the numbers.

      LinkedIn, after the call. Dana, tried you earlier. I work on AI voice and text for charged-off accounts at credit unions. Your Q2 numbers made me think it could be useful. Happy to share what I found either way.

      Objections

      Each follows three moves: agree, ask a question that gets the real concern, then offer a small test.3 About half of cold call objections are reflex brush-offs, not real objections.

      First meeting: five questions

      • How many accounts do you charge off in a typical month, and what is the average balance?
      • Where do they go today, and what does that partner keep?
      • What comes back in the first 12 months?
      • What happens to the accounts your agency returns as uncollectible?
      • What would your compliance team need to see before a 30-day test?

      Scaling hypothesis

      Win ten direct. Then borrow trust.

      Cold outbound gets the first customers and the proof. After that, the fastest growth in this market comes through the organizations credit unions already buy from.

      January to March

      Direct

      Tier A credit unions, one seller, success-fee pilots. Goal: ten paying accounts and one written case study.

      From the first case study

      Partners

      Sell through the groups credit unions already trust. One yes can open dozens of accounts.

      Once the product is proven

      White label

      License the AI to small collection agencies. They keep their clients, you power the calls and take a share.

      Second half of the year

      Next verticals

      Buy-here-pay-here dealers and independent auto lenders, using the same playbook and proof.

      Partner types

      • Credit union service organizations. Companies owned by groups of credit unions to share vendors. Some invest in fintechs directly.1
      • State credit union leagues. Trade groups that endorse vendors. The California league has partnered with collection agencies for its members before.2
      • Core system marketplaces. Credit unions run on a few core banking systems. Being a listed integration, as other AI vendors have done with Jack Henry, removes an IT objection.3
      • Dealer associations and software. For the second vertical, the independent dealers association and dealer software vendors already bundle partner products.4

      How partners get paid

      • Referral share. A cut of your success fee on every account they bring, for the first year. Simple for them to say yes to.
      • Activate early. Most partners send nothing until money lands once. Write their intro email for them and close one deal fast.

      A possible edge

      Spanish. Hispanic-serving credit unions need bilingual collections and few AI vendors lead with it. A team that already builds for Portuguese may handle Spanish better than most. to test with you

      Voice plus text. Nearly half of financially stressed borrowers avoid phone calls, and 81% of credit union members want to resolve late payments on mobile.5 You already do both.
